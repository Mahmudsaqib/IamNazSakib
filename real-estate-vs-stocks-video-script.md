### SCENE 01 — COLD OPEN / TITLE  · weight 0.9
**CAMERA:** Extreme slow push-in from wide to medium on a night skyline; title lands at cut-in.
**VISUAL:** City silhouette, one window lit. Neon "FOR RENT" sign flickers. Title slams in: **REAL ESTATE vs STOCKS** — gold VS badge; subtitle: *The Real Math*.
**SFX:** Low rumble → single deep impact on title.

**VO:** Real estate or stocks — which one actually makes you richer? Forget the opinions. Forget the hype. Tonight we run the real numbers, over twenty years, and the ending surprises almost everyone.

### SCENE 02 — THE 2 A.M. PHONE CALL  · weight 1.0
**CAMERA:** Handheld-feel jitter (2 px shake); tight close-up on a ringing phone, rack focus to a water-stained ceiling.
**VISUAL:** Phone glows in the dark: *INCOMING: TENANT*. Ripple rings. Cut-in: dripping ceiling icon; a red "PROBLEM" tag stamps on screen.
**SFX:** Phone buzz ×3; drip echo.

**VO:** It's two in the morning. The phone rings. A tenant says something in your house just broke. That… is real estate investing. But hold on — this story has two heroes, and the other one is sleeping peacefully.

### SCENE 03 — MEET ETHAN & LUCAS  · weight 1.1
**CAMERA:** Symmetrical two-shot, center split-screen; slow lateral pan left→right introducing each character.
**VISUAL:** Left: **ETHAN** silhouette in gold with a house badge — *28 · $65,000/yr · $50,000 saved*. Right: **LUCAS** silhouette in teal with a chart badge — identical stats. Lower-third: "Same money. Different beliefs."
**SFX:** Soft dual whoosh as each card slides in.

**VO:** Meet Ethan and Lucas. Two friends, twenty-eight years old, both earning sixty-five thousand dollars a year. For years they lived below their means — used cars, no luxury vacations — until each saved fifty thousand dollars. The biggest milestone of their lives.

---

### SCENE 04 — TWO PHILOSOPHIES  · weight 0.8
**CAMERA:** Split-screen dolly: left frame inches forward (property), right frame drifts upward (growth).
**VISUAL:** Left: a solid gold house, heavy and grounded. Right: a thin teal line quietly curving up into the dark. Caption: *Tangible vs Invisible.*
**SFX:** Deep pad note; faint rising shimmer on the right.

**VO:** Ethan wants something he can touch. Lucas believes the best investment is the one that grows in silence, without stealing a single minute of his life. Two philosophies. Twenty years. One winner nobody expects.

### SCENE 05 — ETHAN BUYS THE HOUSE  · weight 1.3
**CAMERA:** Crane-down onto a suburban house; cut-in to a "contract" macro shot where numbers stamp one by one.
**VISUAL:** House priced **$250,000**; down-payment block drops: **−$50,000 (20%)**; mortgage meter fills: **30-year · 7% · $1,330/mo**.
**SFX:** Stamp-thud on each number; pen scratch.

**VO:** Ethan puts his fifty thousand down — twenty percent — on a two-hundred-fifty-thousand-dollar rental home. A humble, well-kept house on a decent street. He finances it with a thirty-year mortgage at seven percent. His payment: about one thousand three hundred thirty dollars a month.

---

### SCENE 06 — THE TENANT & THE "TOO EASY" MATH  · weight 1.0
**CAMERA:** Push-in on a calculator screen; reflection of Ethan smiling faintly in the glass.
**VISUAL:** Green **+$1,800 RENT** flies in, red **−$1,330 MORTGAGE** drops out; big gold remainder counts up: **≈ +$470/mo**. Tag: *Feels too easy.*
**SFX:** Cash-register chime on the remainder.

**VO:** Weeks later, a tenant appears, paying eighteen hundred dollars a month. Ethan does the math and smiles: eighteen hundred in, thirteen thirty out… roughly four hundred seventy dollars of monthly profit. "This is too easy," he thinks.

---

### SCENE 07 — LUCAS GOES INVISIBLE  · weight 1.0
**CAMERA:** Static overhead top-shot of a phone on a desk; slow zoom out revealing Lucas walking away, coffee in hand.
**VISUAL:** **$50,000 → TOTAL MARKET INDEX FUND**; an auto-pay toggle flips ON: **$200/mo**; app closes; screen dims to black. Caption: *No leverage. No tenants. No repairs.*
**SFX:** Single soft tap; ambient room tone.

**VO:** Lucas takes the opposite path. The same fifty thousand goes into a total-market index fund. Two hundred dollars a month, automated. Then he does the hardest thing in investing — he closes the app and gets on with his life.

---

### SCENE 08 — DAY ONE: WINNER vs INVISIBLE  · weight 0.9
**CAMERA:** Face-off composition; slow push on Ethan's proud silhouette, then rack focus to Lucas's faint teal dot.
**VISUAL:** Ethan card: *HOUSE · TENANT · STORY TO TELL*. Lucas card: *an email + a number*. Caption: **One feels real. One feels invisible.**
**SFX:** Heartbeat thump on the caption.

**VO:** On day one, Ethan is the obvious winner. He owns a house. He has a tenant. Lucas has a confirmation email and a number on a screen. But investing isn't about what impresses at Friday dinner — it's about what actually builds wealth.

### SCENE 09 — THE FIRST YEARS (ETHAN'S HIGH)  · weight 1.0
**CAMERA:** Golden-hour drive-by shot past the house; dashboard POV; cut-in to equity bar slowly filling.
**VISUAL:** Rent chips stack monthly; equity gauge **0 % → 12 %**; small plus signs rise from the roof. Ethan whispers: *"I own that."*
**SFX:** Warm acoustic pulse; coin ticks.

**VO:** The early years are kind to Ethan. Rent arrives on time, the loan shrinks, the house quietly rises in value. Every month he owns a little more of it. Every signal tells him he was right.

### SCENE 10 — LUCAS'S BORING DASHBOARD  · weight 0.9
**CAMERA:** Locked-off shot; nothing moves but a small number — the loneliness of compounding.
**VISUAL:** A pale balance flickers up/down: *+$1,140 … −$830 … +$2,050*. Friends' chat bubbles ask about "the house" — none ask about the fund. Caption: **Patient.**
**SFX:** Near silence; occasional soft tick.

**VO:** Lucas feels nothing. Some months up, some down. No one thanks him. No one asks about his account. From the outside, Ethan is the success story. Lucas just looks… patient.

### SCENE 11 — THREE YEARS: PAPER PROFIT  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Cut-in to a floating ledger; camera orbits as numbers stamp into columns.
**VISUAL:** Column A: **RENT $64,800**. Column B: **MORTGAGE −$47,880**. Result stamp: **≈ +$17,000 (on paper)** — the word *paper* glows warning-amber.
**SFX:** Typewriter stamps; subtle alarm seed.

**VO:** Three years in, Ethan has collected sixty-four thousand eight hundred in rent and paid forty-seven thousand eight hundred eighty in mortgage. On paper: seventeen thousand dollars of profit. But there's a problem — that number only exists on paper.

---

### SCENE 12 — THE BILLS FIND THE DOOR  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Handheld push through a front door; bills fly at the lens.
**VISUAL:** Icons swarm in: **TAXES · INSURANCE · MAINTENANCE · 1 MONTH VACANT · LISTING ADS**. The $17,000 number erodes digit by digit → **a few thousand**. Then a second meter appears: **ETHAN'S TIME — unpaid**.
**SFX:** Paper flutter; cash-shred texture.

**VO:** Because the moment you own a rental, the bills find the door: property taxes, insurance, repairs, a month with no tenant, ads to find the next one. Alone, none of them kills you. Together they eat the profit bite by bite — and Ethan's own hours never even entered the equation.

### SCENE 13 — LUCAS AT $76,000  · weight 0.8
**CAMERA:** Slow pull-out from a calm phone screen to Lucas sleeping peacefully.
**VISUAL:** Teal balance rises to **$76,000** (≈10 % avg). Checklist stamps: *No midnight calls ✓ · No repairs ✓*. 
**SFX:** Soft ascending chime.

**VO:** Meanwhile Lucas — assuming the market's long-term average of about ten percent a year — sits near seventy-six thousand dollars. No midnight calls. No leaking faucets. His money simply worked while he lived.

### SCENE 14 — THE ACE: LEVERAGE  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Dramatic low-angle hero shot of the house growing huge; cut-in to a balance-scale: small gold block lifts a massive one.
**VISUAL:** **$50,000 (yours) → controls $250,000**; house value climbs to **$275K–$280K**; equity meter: **$80K–$85K**. Caption: **Leverage — the stock investor's missing superpower.**
**SFX:** Deep heroic swell.

**VO:** But Ethan holds an ace: leverage. Fifty thousand of his own money controls a quarter-million-dollar asset. Three years on, the house is worth around two seventy-five to two-eighty, and his equity has grown to eighty or eighty-five thousand. That power doesn't exist in the stock market — and it built generational fortunes.

### SCENE 15 — THE HIDDEN-COST PLAYBOOK  · weight 1.6
**CAMERA:** Rapid montage; whip-pans between four "cards" (a director's rule-of-four).
**VISUAL:** Card 1 **VACANCY** — calendar loses 1 month/year, mortgage still ticking. Card 2 **MAINTENANCE 1–2 %/yr** — *$2,500–$5,000* on a $250K house; faucet drips into a bucket. Card 3 **BIG REPAIRS** — roof, water heater, furnace: *invoices $$$$*, "not if — when." Card 4 **MANAGEMENT 8–10 %** — a clock melts into dollar bills. 
**SFX:** Four whooshes; drill whir; coin drain.

**VO:** Nobody talks about the playbook. Vacancy: budget one empty month a year — the mortgage still runs. Turnovers: cleaning, paint, ads, screening. Maintenance: one to two percent of value, twenty-five hundred to five thousand a year here. Big repairs: roofs and heaters aren't surprises, they're appointments with destiny. And management costs eight to ten percent of rent — or you pay with your time. Rent minus mortgage is where the story starts, not where it ends.

### SCENE 16 — YEAR FIVE: THE VETERAN  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Time-lapse: same porch, seasons flashing; Ethan ages a touch, shoulders heavier.
**VISUAL:** Tally marks: **3 tenants · 1 roof · 1 plumbing emergency · 2 empty stretches**. Rent rises to **$1,950** — but red bars rise beside it. Final stamp: **cash-on-cash ≈ 3–5 %** vs faded "15–20 % claimed online" crossed out.
**SFX:** Calendar flips; wind.

**VO:** Five years in, Ethan is a veteran. Three tenants, a replaced roof, a plumbing nightmare, empty months. Rent is up to nineteen fifty — but so are taxes, insurance, everything. His real cash-on-cash return: three to five percent. Respectable — nowhere near the fifteen-to-twenty the internet promised.

### SCENE 17 — YEAR SEVEN: COMPOUNDING SHIFTS GEARS  · weight 1.1
**CAMERA:** Macro shot of a teal curve bending; camera tilts up following it as it steepens.
**VISUAL:** Portfolio passes **$120,000**; a "crossover" spark: **annual gains > annual contributions**. Caption: *Gains start having babies.* The curve steepens like a mountain road.
**SFX:** Engine downshift → rising harmony.

**VO:** Lucas's story stays hilariously boring — two hundred a month, no exceptions. By year seven: about a hundred twenty thousand. Then the magic nobody feels: his gains now out-earn his contributions. Compounding just changed gears. Gains have children, then grandchildren — and the curve turns into a mountain.

---

### SCENE 18 — YEAR TEN: PHOTO FINISH  · weight 1.5
**CAMERA:** Split-screen ring-light boxing match framing; both bars rise side by side, camera cutting between them.
**VISUAL:** Ethan: house **$335K–$370K**, loan **−$172K**, equity **$165K–$200K** … plus a red receipt: **−$30,000 costs (10 yrs)**. Lucas: **$170K–$180K**, of which only **$74,000** was his own money. Scoreboard: **≈ TIED**.
**SFX:** Boxing-bell ping on "TIED".

**VO:** Year ten. Ethan's place is worth three thirty-five to three-seventy; after the remaining one-seventy-two loan he holds one sixty-five to two hundred in equity — impressive, but he burned thirty thousand along the way. Lucas stands at one seventy to one-eighty, having only put in seventy-four thousand. They're practically tied. One is chained to one house, one neighborhood, hundreds of hours, six figures of debt. The other owns slivers of thousands of companies — sellable in seconds. Nobody won. They just took opposite roads to the same town.

### SCENE 19 — THE LAST DECADE  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Long slow dolly forward; dust and light rays; a "20 YEARS LATER" title card materializes like a movie interlude.
**VISUAL:** Ethan's property compounds gently to **≈ $500,000**; equity counter climbs to **$385,000**; a second counter totals **+$120,000 net cash flow (20 yrs)**.
**SFX:** Time-passage drone; single piano note.

**VO:** Ten more years. Ethan's house approaches five hundred thousand; his equity, roughly three eighty-five. Across twenty years the property also produced about one hundred twenty thousand in net cash flow after every expense. Real estate, always: it depends on the market, the tenants, and the owner's grind.

### SCENE 20 — LUCAS: THE QUIET MACHINE  · weight 1.0
**CAMERA:** Top-down shot of the untouched phone; the number glows and lifts off the screen into the dark like a lantern.
**VISUAL:** **$518,000** in teal; breakdown bar: **$98,000 you · $420,000+ compounding**. Checklist: *No debt ✓ No tenants ✓ No roofs ✓*.
**SFX:** Warm chime cascade.

**VO:** Lucas just lived the twenty most boring — and most profitable — years of his life. Fifty thousand, two hundred a month, ten percent average: five hundred eighteen thousand dollars. Only ninety-eight thousand left his pocket. The other four hundred twenty thousand? Pure mathematics. No debt. No tenants. No roofs.

### SCENE 21 — THE REVEAL  · weight 1.2
**CAMERA:** Slow push-in on two bars under a single spotlight; the difference glows at the top like a photo-finish photo strip.
**VISUAL:** **ETHAN $505,000** (gold) vs **LUCAS $518,000** (teal). The gap bar ($13K) blinks. Caption: **Two opposite paths. Almost the same destination.**
**SFX:** Heartbeat → silence for one beat.

**VO:** So here's the ending nobody expects. Ethan finishes with about five hundred five thousand. Lucas: five hundred eighteen. Two radically different journeys… nearly the same destination. Ethan built with leverage. Lucas built with time. One carried concentration risk, the other market volatility. Neither chose wrong. They simply chose different.

### SCENE 22 — BUT WAIT: STOCKS HAVE TEETH  · weight 1.6
**CAMERA:** Sudden handheld chaos; red candles crash downward past the lens; camera whip-pans between crash, dividend slip, and a chain labeled LEVERAGE that stocks don't have.
**VISUAL:** Chart 2008: **−40 %**. Chart 2020: **−30 % in weeks**. A portfolio of **$150,000** drips out **≈ $250/mo** in dividends. A "LEVERAGE" harness appears on Ethan's house — nothing on Lucas's chart.
**SFX:** Glass crack; alarm pulse; paper slip.

**VO:** And don't crown stocks just yet — their risks are just quieter. Two thousand eight: down almost forty percent. Early twenty twenty: minus thirty in weeks. Imagine watching years of savings evaporate before breakfast. Dividends? A hundred-fifty-K portfolio drips out maybe two hundred fifty a month — useful, not a salary. And leverage, Ethan's superpower, simply doesn't exist for index investors.

---

### SCENE 23 — THE REAL WINNER  · weight 1.6
**CAMERA:** Both characters step into frame, face the camera; slow push to a black card as the music resolves.
**VISUAL:** "Which wins?" gets a red ✗ stamp. Replacement card: **"The winner is whoever counts EVERYTHING — money, time, stress, risk, opportunity cost."**
**SFX:** Stamp; deep calm bass note.

**VO:** So who actually won? Wrong question. Ethan believed property was the guaranteed road; Lucas believed the market would do everything alone. Both were half right, half wrong — because the real winner is decided by understanding everything that comes with the choice: the money, the time, the stress, the risk, and the opportunity cost.

---

### SCENE 24 — OUTRO: THE ONLY QUESTION  · weight 1.6
**CAMERA:** Ultra-slow pull-back to black; a single gold cursor blinks, then types the final line letter by letter.
**VISUAL:** Final card, centered: **"Did you count everything?"** Below, small: *Only the answer that includes everything truly matters.* CTA: Subscribe — Logical Money.
**SFX:** Typewriter ticks; soft outro chord.

**VO:** There is no perfect investment — only the one that fits your goals, your temperament, and the life you want. So next time someone swears real estate always beats stocks, or stocks always beat real estate, ask them one simple question: did you count everything? Because only the answer that includes everything… is the only answer that matters.

- Each scene animates with a Ken Burns move (push-in / pull-out / pan) plus one cheap live element (counter, bar, curve) per frame.
- One mid-scene cut-in at 60 % simulates a second camera angle.
- Crossfade 0.4 s before Scenes 02, 21, 24; hard cuts elsewhere.
- Safe margins: 5 % from all edges; captions ≥ 42 px.
